\subsection*{Executive Summary}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Enrollment: First-time, full-time undergraduate enrollment in the
    defined public two-year subgroup changed by \EnrollmentChangePct{}\%
    between academic years \AcademicYear{\StudyStartYear} and
    \AcademicYear{\StudyEndYear} within the retained panel.
    \item Grant allocation: The observed and simulated cross-state 90/10
    ratios of aid per enrolled student are \CurrentAidRatio{} and
    \SimulatedAidRatio{}, respectively. Holding enrollment fixed, simulated
    minus observed grant spending is \$\BudgetChangeMillions{} million in
    the retained sample for \AidAcademicYear{}.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Data \& Methodology}
The analysis uses a complete-case balanced panel containing
\PanelInstitutions{} undergraduate institutions and \PanelRows{}
institution-year observations from unrevised IPEDS files. The study covers
academic years \AcademicYear{\StudyStartYear} through
\AcademicYear{\StudyEndYear} and restricts geography to the 50 states;
Washington, DC, and the other excluded areas are outside the study scope.
Year labels in the enrollment figure denote the academic year's starting year.

For enrollment (\texttt{SCUGFFN}) and federal grants (\texttt{FGRNT\_T}),
values with corresponding item-status codes J, L, N, or P are treated as
missing. This is not an R-only restriction on all fields. Institutions are
retained only if all required variables are observed and the geographic,
undergraduate, and classification restrictions are satisfied in every year.
The results therefore describe the retained institutions rather than the full
population. The analysis does not establish whether this selection overstates
or understates the population-wide enrollment change.

The assignment's ``public two-year'' category is operationalized as public
undergraduate institutions with \texttt{CONTROL} equal to 1 and
\texttt{ICLEVEL} in $\{2,3\}$. Institutional level is used as a proxy for the
assignment's non-bachelor's-granting definition; the subgroup is not limited
to institutions coded \texttt{ICLEVEL} equal to 2. Subgroup membership is
selected separately in each year, so the balanced institution panel does not
necessarily imply an unchanged set of public two-year institutions.

\subsection*{2-Year Public College Enrollment Trends}
Total first-time, full-time undergraduate enrollment in the defined public
two-year subgroup was \EnrollmentStart{} in
\AcademicYear{\StudyStartYear} and \EnrollmentEnd{} in
\AcademicYear{\StudyEndYear}, a change of \EnrollmentChangePct{}\%
(Figure~\ref{fig:enrollment}). These totals measure the specified entering
cohort, not all enrolled students. The comparison is descriptive: it does not
identify the causes of the change or establish a long-term structural trend.

\subsection*{Current Federal Grant Distribution (\AidAcademicYear)}
The grant analysis includes all retained undergraduate institutions in
\AidAcademicYear{}, not only the public two-year subgroup. Each state rate is
total federal grant aid for the measured first-time, full-time undergraduate
cohort divided by total enrollment in that cohort at retained institutions in
the state. The denominator includes students not awarded federal grants;
this is not the average grant among recipients. States identify institutional
locations, not students' states of residence. Dollar amounts are not adjusted
for inflation.

Aid per enrolled student was \$\NYAid{} in New York and \$\VTAid{} in
Vermont (Figure~\ref{fig:ny-vt}). Across the \PanelStates{} included states,
the unweighted mean of state rates was \$\CurrentAidMean{}, the sample
standard deviation was \$\CurrentAidSD{}, and the 90/10 ratio was
\CurrentAidRatio{} (Figure~\ref{fig:state-aid}). States receive equal weight
in these statistics; the mean is not the pooled, enrollment-weighted average.
The 90/10 ratio is the 90th percentile divided by the 10th percentile of state
rates, with linear percentile interpolation. Standard deviations use the
sample formula. These differences do not, by themselves, establish geographic
favoritism or inequity; the comparisons do not adjust for student need,
eligibility, or institutional composition.

\subsection*{Simulated Policy Impact}
The simulation applies the specified allocation formula at the institution
level:
\[
    G_i^{\mathrm{sim}} = 1750N_i + 0.15N_i^2,
\]
where $N_i$ is first-time, full-time undergraduate enrollment at institution
$i$ and $G_i^{\mathrm{sim}}$ is its simulated federal grant amount in dollars.
Observed \AidAcademicYear{} enrollment and the retained institutional sample
are held fixed. The allocation is not rescaled to preserve the observed total
budget. The exercise does not model changes in enrollment, institutional
behavior, or implementation expenses.

The observed and simulated standard deviations of state aid rates are
\$\CurrentAidSD{} and \$\SimulatedAidSD{}, respectively. The corresponding
highest-minus-lowest ranges are \$\CurrentAidRange{} and
\$\SimulatedAidRange{}, and the 90/10 ratios are \CurrentAidRatio{} and
\SimulatedAidRatio{} (Figures~\ref{fig:simulated-aid}
and~\ref{fig:policy-spread}). These are comparisons of cross-state dispersion,
not a welfare or equity assessment.

Simulated minus observed grant spending is \$\BudgetChangeDollars{}
(\$\BudgetChangeMillions{} million) within the retained sample for
\AidAcademicYear{}. Positive values indicate additional grant spending;
negative values indicate lower grant spending. This is a single-year,
sample-specific difference, not a cumulative amount or a nationwide
implementation-cost estimate. Figure~\ref{fig:winners-losers} identifies
states with increases and decreases in grant totals at their retained
institutions, rather than states that are better or worse off overall.

\subsection*{Conclusion}
The analysis documents enrollment changes within the retained panel and
compares observed grant allocations with a formula-based, fixed-enrollment
simulation. The findings describe cross-state dispersion and simulated grant
spending within that sample. They do not establish the causes of enrollment
changes, the direction of selection bias, or the welfare effects of the
alternative allocation.
